    Water balance issues

    title: Water balance issues description: Causes of an implausible SWAT+ 62.0.1 water balance, by term: precipitation, runoff, ET, management, percolation, lateral and tile flow, and basin totals. status: review verified_against: - src/climate_control.f90 - src/cli_precip_control.f90 - src/cli_staread.f90 - src/hru_read.f90 - src/hru_module.f90 - src/landuse_read.f90 - src/basin_output.f90 - src/lsu_output.f90 - src/read_mgtops.f90 - src/mgt_sched.f90 - src/topohyd_init.f90 - src/swr_percmicro.f90 - src/hru_output.f90 verified_at_commit: 6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679

    Purpose¶

    When a run completes but basin_wb_aa.txt or hru_wb_aa.txt looks wrong, the cause is usually in the climate inputs, the curve numbers, the management, the soil and hydrology parameters, or the way the basin totals are weighted. This page goes through the water balance term by term. Many of the causes leave no message, so the checks below name the file and column to look at.

    Before changing parameters, read diagnostics.out and checker.out (see Checker and diagnostics) and the list of names matched without a message. An HRU that silently uses the first record of landuse.lum, soils.sol or hydrology.hyd gives a water balance that no parameter change will fix.

    Balance check¶

    For an HRU, landscape unit or the basin, the average annual water balance should close:

    \[P + I + Q_{\mathrm{runon}} \approx ET + WYLD + PERC + \Delta SW + \Delta SNO\]

    where \(P\) is precip, \(I\) is irr, \(Q_{\mathrm{runon}}\) is surq_runon + latq_runon, \(ET\) is et, \(WYLD\) is wateryld (surq_gen + latq + qtile), \(PERC\) is perc, and \(\Delta SW\) and \(\Delta SNO\) are the changes in soil water and snow over the period. The columns are described in Water balance outputs. A large gap points to wetland storage, gwflow exchange or water held in the surface runoff lag. A term that is far from what the climate and land cover suggest points to one of the sections below.

    Precipitation¶

    The precip column does not match the observed rainfall.

    • Weather station of the HRU. Each HRU takes its station from the wst column of hru.con. A name that is not in weather-sta.cli, or a weather-sta.cli whose stations are not in ascending order of name, gives the HRU the first station in the file, with no message. See Weather station lookup.
    • Data file of the station. A line file not found (pgage) in diagnostics.out means the station names a precipitation file that is not listed in pcp.cli, and SWAT+ generates the rainfall from the weather generator on every day. See Weather station names.
    • Simulation period. Days before the start or after the end of the record, and days flagged as missing, are generated. See Weather outside the observed record.
    • Sub-daily runs. With step above 1 in time.sim, a station with daily precipitation records gets no rainfall in its time steps. A sub-daily run needs sub-daily records or generated precipitation. See Time step.

    hru_pw files and the precip column of hru_wb show what each HRU received, so compare a few HRUs with the station file before looking further.

    Surface runoff¶

    surq_gen is too high or too low.

    • Curve numbers. The cn2 column of landuse.lum names a record in cntable.lum, and the soil group in soils.sol picks one of its four values. A name that is not found is reported as not found in cntable.lum, and the HRU uses the built-in values 30, 55, 70 and 77 for groups A to D. The hydgrp column of checker.out shows the soil group each HRU uses.
    • Moisture adjustment. cn3_swf in hydrology.hyd places the curve number for wet conditions between field capacity (0) and saturation (1). See Curve number soil water factor.
    • Runoff method. gampt in codes.bsn selects the curve number method (0) or Green-Ampt (1), which is meant for sub-daily runs. See Runoff method.
    • Lag. surq_gen is the runoff after the surface runoff lag and surq_cont the runoff generated on the day. A large difference between them in yearly totals means water is held in the lag.

    Evapotranspiration¶

    et is too high or too low.

    • PET method. pet in codes.bsn: 0 Priestley-Taylor, 1 Penman-Monteith, 2 Hargreaves, 3 the PET file of the station. Penman-Monteith uses solar radiation, humidity and wind, which are generated when the station has no records for them. Compare the pet column of the water balance file with a regional estimate.
    • Compensation factors. esco and epco in hydrology.hyd set how far down the profile soil evaporation and plant uptake can reach. checker.out lists the values each HRU uses. See Soil evaporation.
    • Plant cover. If plants do not grow, eplant stays low and more water leaves as soil evaporation, runoff and percolation. Check biomass and LAI in the hru_pw files and yields in crop_yld_aa.txt (see Plant and weather).

    Management¶

    Crops do not grow, or grow without fertilizer or harvest.

    • A land use whose mgt schedule is not in management.sch is reported as not found in management.sch, and its HRUs get no operations. The Ames_sub1 reference dataset has four such land uses.
    • Several operation names are not reported when they are not found: a fertilizer name gives no fertilizer, a harvest operation no harvest, a tillage name no mixing, and a transplant name a plant started from seed. In the Ames_sub1 reference dataset, the fertilizer names of every fert line are in the wrong column, so the dataset applies no mineral fertilizer in 62.0.1. See the management.sch example.
    • Set mgtout to y in print.prt to write mgt_out.txt, the log of every operation and decision table action that ran. An operation missing from the log did not happen.

    Percolation¶

    perc is close to zero.

    perco in hydrology.hyd limits percolation out of the bottom of the soil profile, and the relation is strongly non-linear: 0.5 lets about 0.5 % of the potential percolation through, 0.9 about 3 %, and only values close to 1 allow free drainage. The value 0.5, used for every HRU in Ames_sub1, therefore leaves almost nothing to percolate. See Percolation from the soil profile.

    Also check sumfc and sumul in checker.out, the water held at field capacity and saturation over the profile. Values that are the same for every HRU can mean the HRUs all use the first record of soils.sol because their soil names did not match.

    Lateral and tile flow¶

    latq or qtile is out of range.

    • Lateral flow. latq_co and lat_ttime in hydrology.hyd scale and delay lateral flow. See Lateral flow.
    • Tile drains. An HRU has a tile drain when the tile column of its land use names a record in tiledrain.str. The tiledrain column of checker.out shows 1 for those HRUs. A decision table tile_install action has no effect on an HRU whose land use had no tile record at the start. A tile lag of 0.01 h or less keeps tile water in the lag store, so qtile stays at zero. See Tile flow methods.
    • Septic systems. In 62.0.1 a septic system adds no water or nutrients to the soil. See Septic simulation in 62.0.1.

    Basin totals¶

    The basin or landscape unit values do not match the HRU values.

    Basin rows are sums of the HRU values weighted by the BSN_FRAC column of ls_unit.ele, and landscape unit rows by RU_FRAC. 62.0.1 uses these fractions as given, without scaling them to add up to 1. In the Ames_sub1 reference dataset the 12 BSN_FRAC values add up to 2, so every basin water balance, nutrient balance, losses and plant file shows twice the area-weighted HRU average. Set each fraction to the HRU area divided by the basin area, and check that they add up to 1. The basin sum also assumes ls_unit.ele lists one element per HRU, in HRU order. See Aggregation and Nutrient balance and losses.

    Routing unit hydrograph files (ru_*) add up every hydrograph type of every element: total flow plus the recharge, surface, lateral and tile parts. Their flo is therefore larger than the routing unit's outflow, which is in the hydout files. See ru files.

    flo in the hydrograph files is a volume in m³ per print step, although the units row gives m³/s. Divide a daily value by 86,400 to compare it with a gauge record. See Hydrograph columns.
