    Nutrient cycling

    Overview¶

    SWAT+ follows nitrogen and phosphorus through mineral, organic and plant pools in every soil layer. Fertiliser, manure, residue and atmospheric deposition add nutrients; plants take them up; and runoff, sediment, lateral flow, tile flow, percolation, denitrification and volatilisation remove them. The nutrients that leave the HRU are routed through channels, where in-stream processes are described on the Channels page.

    The soil processes below are those of the static carbon option (carbon = 0 in codes.bsn). With carbon = 2 the residue and humus pools and their decomposition follow the CENTURY-based model on the Carbon page, while nitrification, volatilisation, mineral phosphorus, deposition and all the transport processes below stay the same. carbon = 1 is reserved for C-FARM, which is not implemented; with it SWAT+ 62.0.1 skips residue decomposition and mineralisation altogether.

    Plant uptake and nitrogen fixation are described in Plant growth and land use management.

    Soil pools¶

    Element Pool Contents
    N Nitrate NO3-N in solution, mobile with water
    N Ammonium NH4-N, adsorbed on the soil
    N Active humus N The part of humus organic N that mineralises
    N Stable humus N The rest of humus organic N
    N Residue N Organic N in fresh residue, kept separately for each plant in each layer and on the surface
    P Labile (solution) P Soluble mineral P available to plants and mobile with water
    P Active mineral P Mineral P in rapid equilibrium with labile P
    P Stable mineral P Mineral P in slow equilibrium with active P
    P Humus organic P Organic P in humus
    P Residue P Organic P in fresh residue

    Manure applied to the soil has separate organic pools, which are also lost with sediment.

    Initial values¶

    The initial nutrient contents come from the row of nutrients.sol named for the HRU in soil_plant.ini, with built-in values when there is none:

    Pool Initial value
    Nitrate nitrate (7 mg/kg) at the surface, decreasing with depth as \(\exp(-\mathrm{exp\_co}\,z)\), with \(z\) the depth to the bottom of the layer in mm
    Labile P lab_p (5 mg/kg) at the surface, decreasing with depth the same way
    Active mineral P Labile P × (1 − PSP)/PSP
    Stable mineral P 4 × active P; with the Vadas and White P model, from the White et al. (2009) ratio of 1 to 7 times active plus labile P
    Humus C, N and P Humus C from the organic carbon of the layer (carbon in soils.sol); N and P from the humus C:N and C:P ratios hum_c_n (10) and hum_c_p (80)
    Active and stable humus fr_hum_act (0.02) of humus in the active pool

    Concentrations in mg/kg are converted to kg/ha with the bulk density and thickness of each layer. The other soil test columns of nutrients.sol (inorgp, watersol_p, h3a_p, mehlich_p, bray_strong_p) are not used.

    Temperature and water factors¶

    Mineralisation, decomposition and denitrification depend on soil temperature and water through two factors:

    \[\gamma_{tmp} = \max\left(0.1,\; 0.9\,\frac{T_{soil}}{T_{soil} + \exp(9.93 - 0.312\,T_{soil})} + 0.1\right), \qquad \gamma_{sw} = \max\left(0.05,\; 0.1 + 0.9\sqrt{\frac{SW_{ly}}{FC_{ly}}}\right)\]

    where \(T_{soil}\) is the layer temperature (°C), \(SW_{ly}\) the layer water and \(FC_{ly}\) its field capacity water. They combine as \(\gamma = \sqrt{\gamma_{tmp}\,\gamma_{sw}}\). None of these processes runs in a layer at or below 0 °C. For the 10 mm surface layer, SWAT+ uses the temperature and water content of the second layer.

    Humus mineralisation¶

    Nitrogen moves between the active and stable humus pools towards a fixed active fraction:

    \[N_{trns} = 10^{-5}\left[orgN_{act}\left(\frac{1}{fr_{actN}} - 1\right) - orgN_{sta}\right]\]

    where \(fr_{actN}\) is n_act_frac in carbon.bsn (0.02). A positive value moves N from active to stable humus.

    Active humus N mineralises to nitrate:

    \[N_{mina} = \beta_{min}\,\gamma\,orgN_{act}\]

    with \(\beta_{min}\) the rate coefficient for humus mineralisation, orgn_min in parameters.bsn. Phosphorus is mineralised from humus in proportion:

    \[P_{mina} = 1.4\,N_{mina}\,\frac{orgP_{hum}}{orgN_{act} + orgN_{sta}}\]

    and added to the labile pool.

    Residue decomposition¶

    Fresh residue decomposes at a rate limited by its C:N and C:P ratios. For each plant's residue in each layer,

    \[\gamma_{ntr} = \min\left(\exp\left[-0.693\,\frac{\varepsilon_{C:N} - 25}{25}\right],\; \exp\left[-0.693\,\frac{\varepsilon_{C:P} - 200}{200}\right],\; 1\right)\]

    with \(\varepsilon_{C:N}\) capped at 500 and \(\varepsilon_{C:P}\) at 5000. The fraction decomposed on the day is

    \[\delta_{ntr} = \min\left[1,\; \max\left(\mathrm{rsd\_decay},\; \beta_{rsd}\,\gamma_{ntr}\,\gamma\right)\right]\]

    where \(\beta_{rsd}\) is the residue decomposition coefficient of the plant (plnt_decomp in plants.plt) and rsd_decay in parameters.bsn is a minimum daily rate. Of the N and P released, 80% goes to nitrate and labile P, and 20% to humus (active humus N; stable humus P for soil residue, active humus P for surface residue).

    Surface residue decomposes the same way, using the temperature and water of the top layer.

    Nitrification and ammonia volatilisation¶

    Ammonium is nitrified and volatilised together in each layer (Reddy et al., 1979; Godwin et al., 1984). The temperature factor is

    \[\eta_{tmp} = 0.41\,\frac{T_{soil} - 5}{10}\]

    and both processes need \(\eta_{tmp} \ge 0.001\). The water factor for nitrification rises linearly from 0 at wilting point to 1 at wilting point plus 25% of the field capacity water. Volatilisation decreases with depth through

    \[\eta_{midz} = 1 - \frac{z_{mid}}{z_{mid} + \exp(4.706 - 0.0305\,z_{mid})}\]

    with \(z_{mid}\) the depth to the middle of the layer (mm), and uses a cation exchange factor of 0.15. With \(\eta_{nit} = \eta_{tmp}\,\eta_{sw}\) and \(\eta_{vol} = 0.15\,\eta_{tmp}\,\eta_{midz}\), the ammonium lost to both is

    \[N_{nit|vol} = NH_4\left[1 - \exp(-\eta_{nit} - \eta_{vol})\right]\]

    and is split between nitrification and volatilisation in proportion to \(1 - \exp(-\eta_{nit})\) and \(1 - \exp(-\eta_{vol})\).

    Denitrification¶

    When the water factor reaches a threshold, nitrate in the layer is denitrified:

    \[N_{denit} = NO_3\left[1 - \exp\left(-\beta_{denit}\,\gamma_{tmp}\,\frac{orgC}{100}\right)\right] \quad\text{for } \gamma_{sw} \ge \gamma_{sw,thr}\]
    Symbol Meaning Input
    \(\beta_{denit}\) Denitrification rate coefficient denit_exp in parameters.bsn
    \(\gamma_{sw,thr}\) Threshold of the water factor denit_frac in parameters.bsn
    \(orgC\) Organic carbon of the layer carbon in soils.sol, %

    The septic biozone layer of an active septic system is excluded.

    Atmospheric deposition¶

    Nitrate and ammonium in rain and dry deposition are added to the surface layer every day from the station named in the atmo_dep column of weather-sta.cli. Rain deposition is the concentration in rain (mg/L) times the day's precipitation; dry deposition is spread evenly over the days of the month or year, depending on whether atmodep.cli gives monthly, yearly or average annual values.

    Nitrate movement¶

    Nitrate is mobile and moves with water. In each layer the water that moves is the percolation, lateral flow, surface runoff (top layer only) and tile flow (tile layer only), \(w_{mobile}\). The nitrate concentration in the mobile water is

    \[conc_{NO_3} = \frac{NO_3\left[1 - \exp\left(\dfrac{-w_{mobile}}{(1 - \theta_e)\,SAT_{ly}}\right)\right]}{w_{mobile}}\]

    where \(\theta_e\) is the fraction of porosity from which anions are excluded (anion_excl in soils.sol) and \(SAT_{ly}\) the saturation water of the layer. Then:

    Path Nitrate removed
    Surface runoff \(\beta_{NO_3}\,conc_{NO_3}\,Q_{surf}\), top layer, with \(\beta_{NO_3}\) = n_perc in parameters.bsn
    Lateral flow \(conc_{NO_3}\,Q_{lat,ly}\) for each layer
    Tile flow nperco_lchtile × concentration in the tile flow × \(Q_{tile}\), with the concentration taken over the tile layer and all layers below
    Percolation \(conc_{NO_3}\,w_{perc,ly}\), added to the next layer; from the bottom layer it leaves the profile

    n_perc close to 0 gives little nitrate in surface runoff; close to 1 gives runoff the same concentration as percolate. As water evaporates from the second layer, a small amount of nitrate moves up with it into the surface layer.

    Nitrate in lateral and tile flow passes through the same lag stores as the water (see Hydrology). When the gwflow module passes groundwater into the soil profile, it also adds the nitrate and phosphorus that the groundwater contains.

    Organic N and P with sediment¶

    Organic nitrogen leaving with sediment is

    \[orgN_{surf} = 1000\,\frac{orgN_{ly1}}{M_{ly1}}\,\varepsilon_N\,\frac{sed}{A}\]
    Symbol Meaning Units
    \(orgN_{surf}\) Organic N removed in sediment kg N/ha
    \(orgN_{ly1}\) Active plus stable humus N in the surface layer kg N/ha
    \(M_{ly1}\) Mass of the surface layer, \(10000\,\rho_b\,depth\) kg/ha
    \(\varepsilon_N\) Nitrogen enrichment ratio -
    \(sed\) Sediment yield metric tons
    \(A\) HRU area ha

    Sediment-attached phosphorus is computed the same way from the humus, manure and active and stable mineral P of the surface layer, using \(\varepsilon_P\), and is reported as organic P and mineral P.

    The enrichment ratio accounts for the finer, nutrient-rich particles that erosion removes. It is orgn_enrich (N) or orgp_enrich (P) in hydrology.hyd when that is above 0.001. Otherwise it is computed from the sediment concentration in runoff (Menzel, 1980):

    \[\varepsilon = 0.78\,(conc_{sed})^{-0.2468}, \qquad conc_{sed} = \frac{sed}{10\,A\,Q_{surf}}, \qquad \varepsilon \le 3\]

    Soluble phosphorus¶

    Labile phosphorus is strongly bound by the soil, so little of it moves with water. The amount removed in surface runoff is

    \[P_{surf} = \frac{P_{lab,1}\,Q_{surf}}{\rho_b\,depth_1\,k_{d,surf} + 1}\]

    where \(P_{lab,1}\) is the labile P of the surface layer (kg/ha), \(\rho_b\) and \(depth_1\) its bulk density and thickness (mm), and \(k_{d,surf}\) the phosphorus soil partitioning coefficient, p_soil in parameters.bsn (m³/Mg).

    Leaching of labile P from each layer to the next is

    \[P_{perc} = 0.001\,P_{lab}\left[1 - \exp\left(\frac{-w_{perc}}{0.01\,SW_{ly} + 0.1\,k_{d,perc}\,\rho_b}\right)\right]\]

    with \(k_{d,perc}\) the phosphorus percolation coefficient p_perc in parameters.bsn. The same expression with tile flow in place of percolation gives the P in tile flow from the tile layer. This form differs from the SWAT 2009 leaching equation.

    Mineral phosphorus transformations¶

    Original model¶

    With soil_p = 0 in codes.bsn, labile and active mineral P move towards the ratio set by the phosphorus availability index \(pai\) (p_avail in parameters.bsn):

    \[P_{sol|act} = P_{lab} - P_{act}\,\frac{pai}{1 - pai}\]

    Movement from labile to active P takes 10% of the imbalance per day; movement back takes 60%. Active and stable P move towards a stable pool four times the active pool:

    \[P_{act|sta} = 0.01\,(4\,P_{act} - P_{sta})\]

    with a tenth of that rate when stable P returns to the active pool.

    Vadas and White model¶

    With soil_p = 1, SWAT+ uses the phosphorus model of Vadas and White (2010). The availability index is computed for each layer from clay content, labile P and organic carbon, and averaged over 30 days. The rate of movement between labile and active P depends on how many days the imbalance has lasted. The active to stable equilibrium ratio follows the White et al. (2009) relationship, and the active to stable rate depends on the calcium carbonate content (caco3 in soils.sol). Both rates are scaled by the water content of the layer relative to field capacity, with a lower limit of 0.25.

    Lag of nutrients in surface runoff¶

    Nitrate and labile P in surface runoff, organic N and P, and mineral P attached to sediment pass through the surface lag store with the runoff and sediment, and the same daily fraction is released (see Hydrology).

    Algae, CBOD and dissolved oxygen in runoff¶

    Each day SWAT+ also gives the water leaving an HRU a chlorophyll-a, carbonaceous BOD and dissolved oxygen load, which the channels then use as inflow concentrations. On days without water yield all three are zero. With water yield \(Q\) (surface runoff, lateral flow and tile flow, mm):

    • Chlorophyll-a is 0.1 times the concentration of organic N in sediment plus nitrate in surface runoff, \(100\,(N_{org,sed} + NO_{3,surq})/Q\).
    • CBOD is 2.7 times the organic carbon in the day's sediment yield, divided by the water yield. The carbon is the soil carbon content of the top layer times the sediment yield and the enrichment ratio, or, with carbon = 2 in codes.bsn, the carbon the carbon model reports in the sediment.
    • Dissolved oxygen is \(O_{sat}\,e^{-0.1\,CBOD}\), limited to between 0 and \(O_{sat}\), with \(O_{sat}\) the saturation concentration at a water temperature of \(5 + 0.75\,\bar{T}_{air}\) (see Channels for the formula).

    Septic systems¶

    A land use with a septic system (the sep column of landuse.lum) adds a biozone layer to the soil, and the biozone routine runs on days when that layer is above freezing. In 62.0.1 the effluent volume is never computed, so septic systems add no water, nitrogen, phosphorus or BOD to the soil. See septic.str for the inputs.

    In-stream processes¶

    Nutrients entering channels are transformed by the QUAL2E water quality routines. With i_fpwet = 1 in codes.bsn (the column read as the QUAL2E switch), a simplified exchange between dissolved and particulate N and P is added. See Channels.

    Switches and parameters¶

    Input File Effect
    carbon codes.bsn 0 static pools, 2 CENTURY-based carbon, 1 reserved
    soil_p codes.bsn 0 original mineral P model, 1 Vadas and White (2010)
    orgn_min parameters.bsn Humus mineralisation rate coefficient
    rsd_decay parameters.bsn Minimum daily residue decomposition
    denit_exp, denit_frac parameters.bsn Denitrification rate and water threshold
    n_perc parameters.bsn Nitrate in surface runoff relative to percolate
    nperco_lchtile parameters.bsn Nitrate concentration coefficient for tile flow
    p_soil, p_perc parameters.bsn Phosphorus partitioning in runoff and percolation
    p_avail parameters.bsn Phosphorus availability index (original model)
    n_act_frac carbon.bsn Active fraction of humus N
    orgn_enrich, orgp_enrich hydrology.hyd Fixed enrichment ratios
    plnt_decomp plants.plt Residue decomposition coefficient of each plant
    nitrate, lab_p, exp_co, fr_hum_act, hum_c_n, hum_c_p nutrients.sol Initial nutrient contents
    carbon, anion_excl, caco3 soils.sol Organic carbon, anion exclusion, CaCO3

    orgn_min, n_perc, nperco_lchtile, p_soil, p_perc and p_avail are copied to every HRU at the start of the run, so calibration can change them for single HRUs.
